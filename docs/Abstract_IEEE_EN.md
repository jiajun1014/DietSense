**Title — DietSense: A Data-Grounded and Safety-Aware Conversational AI Agent for Personalized Dietary Assessment and Weight Management**

**Abstract —** The increasing prevalence of overweight and obesity, affecting an estimated 2.6 billion adults worldwide in 2024, including 940 million individuals living with obesity, underscores the need for sustainable dietary monitoring. However, conventional dietary tracking systems require extensive manual input, including food identification, portion estimation, and ingredient decomposition. Self-reported dietary intake errors ranging from 18% to 54% further complicate nutritional assessment, particularly for mixed dishes and culturally specific meals. Although large language models (LLMs) enable natural-language interaction, their direct use for nutritional estimation introduces risks of hallucinated values, inconsistent calculations, and unsupported recommendations. To address these limitations, this paper proposes DietSense, a data-grounded, safety-aware conversational AI agent that integrates language-based interaction with domain-specific computational services. The proposed system adopts a tool-oriented architecture in which an LLM coordinates information retrieval and analytical operations through whitelisted function calls, while numerical calculations are delegated to deterministic modules. The framework integrates retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), USDA FoodData Central, the Taiwan Food Composition Database, and a dedicated vector database of Taiwanese cuisine. Its eight-module architecture encompasses speech input, intent classification, agent planning, data retrieval, nutritional analysis, domain knowledge integration, recommendation generation, and interactive reporting. A clarification mechanism resolves ambiguities in food identity, portion size, and preparation methods, while uncertainty quantification and rule-based safety constraints support reliable decision-making. Individual energy requirements are estimated using the Mifflin–St Jeor equation and total daily energy expenditure (TDEE) calculations. The evaluation framework will employ 500 annotated Taiwanese food records, including night-market snacks, lunchboxes, noodle soups, mixed dishes, and beverages, supplemented by established nutritional databases. System assessment will examine estimation accuracy, uncertainty handling, interaction efficiency, and safety compliance. DietSense is designed to generate caloric and macronutrient ranges, individualized energy-balance assessments, dietary substitution recommendations, longitudinal trends, and traceable nutritional evidence rather than unsupported point estimates. The proposed architecture establishes a foundation for evaluating whether coordinated AI agents and domain-grounded analytical services can reduce dietary logging burden while improving the transparency, consistency, and safety of personalized nutritional decision support.

**Index Terms —** Conversational AI, domain-specific AI agents, dietary assessment, large language models, retrieval-augmented generation, uncertainty quantification, personalized weight management, safety-aware systems.
